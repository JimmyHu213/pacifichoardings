## 1. Statutory framework — who says you need a permit

**[VERIFIED 3-0] Roads Act 1993 (NSW) s138** is the core power. A person must not "erect a structure or carry out a work in, on or over a public road" without the consent of the appropriate roads authority. Any hoarding standing on or over a footpath/roadway is caught by this. Max penalty: 10 penalty units. *(austlii — Roads Act s138)*

**[VERIFIED 3-0] Local Government Act 1993 (NSW) s68** requires prior council approval for activities in its Table. **Part E (Public roads)** specifically covers swinging/hoisting goods over a public road by a lift/hoist/tackle projecting over the footway — i.e. **craneage/hoisting is a separate approval** from the hoarding itself. *(austlii — LGA s68)*

Nuance worth knowing: **[PRIMARY]** the consolidated s68 Table does **not** contain a line-item for "erect a hoarding" — so the power to approve a hoarding *structure* on/over a road flows from **Roads Act s138**, while s68 covers associated activities (hoisting, waste containers). In practice councils cite **both**.

How approvals are actually granted (City of Sydney model): **[PRIMARY]** issued under **s94 LGA** (as an s68 category E(2) activity) **and s139 Roads Act**, subject to conditions.

**Classified roads:** **[PRIMARY]** on a classified road, s138 consent cannot be given without the **concurrence of Transport for NSW** (s138(2)). So CBD/main-road jobs can involve TfNSW as well as council.

**Consent authority:** **[PRIMARY]** for all unclassified roads and some classified roads, the **local council** is the consent authority; other classified roads also need TfNSW. Section 138 applications can be lodged via the **NSW Planning Portal** for participating councils (80+).

## 2. Class A vs Class B — definitions and terminology

### ⚠️ Key finding: "Class" vs "Type" terminology

The industry and SafeWork NSW say **"Class A / Class B"**; **most NSW councils say "Type A / Type B"** for the same things. This matters for how the site reads to council-side readers.

- **SafeWork NSW** (WHS regulator): uses **Class-A / Class-B**. *[PRIMARY]* "Class-A hoarding: used to secure the perimeter of a worksite… self-supporting. Class-B hoarding: industry term commonly used for an overhead protective structure."
- **City of Sydney, City of Parramatta, Inner West, North Sydney:** all use **Type A / Type B**.
- **Hornsby Shire:** uses **Class A / B / C** (three classes — see §7).

**Recommendation:** the site's "Class A / Class B" language is correct industry/SafeWork usage — keep it, but consider a one-line note that "some councils call these Type A / Type B" so builders aren't confused.

### What each class is

- **Class/Type A (ground-level fence hoarding):** **[PRIMARY]** a plywood-sheet fence on a timber or steel frame separating the worksite from the public. City of Sydney: "2.1m high ply-sheet fence… on a timber or steel frame." Common minimum height **2.0–2.1 m**, solid timber panels **≥17 mm**, accessible footpath maintained **≥1500 mm**.
- **Class/Type B (overhead protective structure / gantry):** **[PRIMARY]** "a more substantial structure that spans a footway or roadway and affords overhead protection to the public" — typically a **prefabricated modular steel gantry** you walk beneath. Minimum overhead clearance **~2.2–2.4 m** depending on council.

### When Class B is triggered (North Sydney & Parramatta rule)

**[PRIMARY]** A Type B / overhead hoarding is required where works to a building adjoining a public way:
- are **7500 mm or greater in height and less than 3500 mm from the street alignment**; or
- have a vertical height above footpath level of less than 4000 mm; or similar least-height triggers.

North Sydney adds: Type B must have **2200 mm min overhead clearance**, **1500 mm clear span**, **prefabricated modular steel gantries** encouraged, **scaffolding or shipping containers NOT permitted** as the gantry.

## 3. Technical standards & engineering — the AS 4687 question

### ⚠️ Key finding: AS 4687 is only part of the picture

The site currently says everything is "engineered to AS 4687." The research shows a more precise position:

- **[PRIMARY] AS 4687 (Series) "Temporary fencing and hoardings"** is the correct standard for **fencing and hoardings generally** — SafeWork NSW's new code explicitly says "any hoardings or fencing should comply with AS 4687 (Series)." So AS 4687 is defensible for **Class A fence-type hoardings**.
- **BUT overhead protective structures (Class B) are structurally designed to AS/NZS 1170 loads**, not AS 4687. Council design documents (City of Sydney 2017 Guidelines, North Sydney) cite **AS 1170 (Parts 1/2/4)**, plus AS 1725, AS 1158, AS 1742 — **and do not cite AS 4687** for overhead structure design.

**Recommendation for site content:** keep AS 4687 for Class A / temporary fencing, but for **Class B** describe it as *"engineered to AS/NZS 1170 load cases and the SafeWork NSW Overhead Protective Structures Code of Practice"* — that is what councils and the regulator actually require, and it reads as more expert, not less.

### ⚠️ Key finding: brand-new codes (Dec 2025) — the site must not cite the 1995 code

- **[PRIMARY] SafeWork NSW "Overhead protective structures" Code of Practice** — approved under **s274 WHS Act 2011**, **commenced 12 December 2025**, **replaces the 1995 code**. Sets **two live-load design classes**: **5 kPa** minimum for minor works (maintenance/cleaning/painting/light swing stage) and **10 kPa** minimum for construction/demolition, designed to AS/NZS 1170. Design should be done/verified by a **structural engineer with ≥5 years' experience on the National Engineers Register**.
- **[PRIMARY] City of Sydney "Code of practice: construction-related temporary structures on and above roads"** — **published 27 Nov 2025**, replaces the 2017 "Guidelines for hoardings and scaffolding." Paired with a **Local Approvals Policy (21 Nov 2022)**.
- NSW **Codes of Practice are automatically admissible as evidence** under the WHS Act — contractors' obligations are benchmarked against them.

### Inspection / certification cadence

**[PRIMARY]** Overhead protective structure (non-scaffold): certified by a competent person (structural engineer) **after installation and at ≥6-monthly intervals**, plus after any event affecting integrity. **Scaffold-based structures: at least every 30 days** (per AS 4576). City of Sydney requires an **installation certificate before use** and **re-certification at intervals of not more than six months**.

## 4. Application requirements & documentation (typical)

**[PRIMARY]** Across councils, a hoarding/s138 application typically needs:

- Completed council application form + fee
- **Scaled site and elevation plans / engineering plans**
- **Structural engineer's certificate** the structure is sound and can carry the loads (Inner West requires **two** — one pre-install/design, one post-install)
- **Public liability insurance — Certificate of Currency, minimum $20,000,000**, in the approval-holder's name, noting the council's interest (confirmed at **City of Sydney, North Sydney, Inner West**)
- **Traffic Management Plan** where pedestrian/vehicle movement is altered (Inner West: **compliant with AS 1742.3**; Parramatta: **prepared by an accredited traffic engineer**)
- Contractor details and estimated cost of works

> **The site's "$20M public liability" claim is well-supported** — three councils independently specify exactly $20,000,000. Good to keep.

- **City of Sydney:** fees by **hoarding length along frontage**, varying by type/location/decking; **performance bond mainly for Type B**; online calculator (estimates only).
- **North Sydney:** approvals capped at **6 months (26 weeks)**; **13-week minimum** charge if in place >90 days.
- **Hornsby:** upfront non-refundable permit fee; total per Fees & Charges by type; type assessed on-site.

## 6. Council-by-council comparison

| Council | Terminology | Class/Type A | Class/Type B trigger & spec | PL insurance | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **City of Sydney** | Type A/B | Ply fence ≥2.0–2.1 m, timber/steel frame | Steel gantry spanning footway, overhead protection | **$20M** | New Code of Practice **27 Nov 2025**; **performance bond for Type B**; **mandatory creative graphics** in some cases; classified-road → RMS/TfNSW concurrence |
| **North Sydney** | Type A/B | ≥2100 mm, timber ≥17 mm, footpath ≥1500 mm | Building **≥7500 mm high & <3500 mm from alignment**; clearance 2200 mm, span 1500 mm; **no scaffold/containers** as gantry | **$20M** | Approval max **6 months**; TMP required; cites AS 1170/1725/1158/1742 (not AS 4687) |
| **Parramatta** | Type A/B | Low-risk zone 1800 mm / high-risk 2100 mm + timber 17 mm | Overhead per height/proximity triggers; clearance 2200 mm, span 1500 mm; complies w/ Overhead Protective Structures COP | (per schedule) | **Type by risk zone** (R1–R3 = low; business/industrial/R4 = high); **Hoarding & Tower Crane Policy 2017**; detailed 2025/26 fees |
| **Inner West** | Type A/B/**C** | Ground-level hoarding/scaffold/fencing | Built over footpath, **≥2.4 m clearance**; **two** eng certs (pre+post) | **$20M** | Type C = light-duty safety fencing; TMP to **AS 1742.3**; 600 mm kerb + 1.5 m footpath clearances |
| **Hornsby** | **Class A/B/C** | Class A = fence **≤300 mm** onto council land | **Class B = solid/overhead structure, largest type**, for pedestrian overhead protection, ±site sheds | (per schedule) | **Three classes by encroachment**; Class C = fence/scaffold **>300 mm–1800 mm** onto council land; type assessed on-site |

**Takeaway:** the A/B split is near-universal but the **triggers, dimensions, terminology and fees differ by council** — which is exactly the "we handle the council paperwork" value proposition. Parramatta's risk-zone approach and Hornsby's three-class-by-encroachment scheme are the notable outliers.

## 7. Implications for the Pacific Hoardings website

Concrete content changes suggested by this research (each would go through a PR):

1. **AS 4687 precision** — keep AS 4687 for Class A/fencing; for **Class B**, cite **AS/NZS 1170 + SafeWork NSW Overhead Protective Structures Code of Practice (commenced 12 Dec 2025)**. More accurate and more expert.
2. **Refresh the compliance page** to reference the **current** codes (SafeWork 12 Dec 2025; City of Sydney Code of Practice 27 Nov 2025) — do **not** cite the superseded 1995 / 2017 documents.
3. **$20M public liability** — well-supported, keep as-is.
4. **Add a "Class vs Type" note** — reassure builders that "Class A/B" = councils' "Type A/B."
5. **Council-specific FAQ or page** — a short "councils we work across / how requirements differ" section using the comparison table would be strong SEO and matches the real differentiator (navigating each council's rules).
6. **Certification cadence** — the compliance page can accurately state "installation certificate before use, re-certified at ≥6-monthly intervals (30-day for scaffold-based per AS 4576)."
7. **Class B trigger** — the "7500 mm high / <3500 mm from alignment" rule is a concrete, credible detail to state on the Class B service page.
